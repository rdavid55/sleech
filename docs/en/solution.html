SLEECH / SOLUTION

Help align inspection results.

For quality teams and metrologists who need to review disagreeing results or agree inspection before delivery.

Problem hypothesis

Requirements exist. The disagreement still needs investigation.

A contract and technical documentation can specify product requirements and complaint procedures. A particular case can still require collecting raw records, checking methods and conditions, and agreeing a repeat inspection.

Frequency, investigation costs and willingness to pay are being validated. We do not assume that company contracts necessarily have gaps.

What the customer receives

  1. A case record and an input checklist.
  2. A map of differences in methods and conditions.
  3. An agreed review plan or draft protocol.
  4. A report on the agreed scope and next actions.

Insufficient evidence can also be a review outcome. A metrological conclusion requires a competent specialist.

01

Describe the task

A short questionnaire identifies the next step. Ambiguous or new tasks go through initial clarification.

02

Agree the scope

The case records the work, deliverables, price and accepted version. The contract defines the provider, deadline, responsibilities and acceptance criteria.

03

Receive the result

Materials, discussion and the final result stay under one case reference. Each company has separate access.

How pricing works

Price depends on the agreed scope: team time, specialist work, external measurements when required and coordination. Characteristics, sites, input completeness, repeated rounds and travel affect the estimate.

The first commercial hypothesis is a one-off service. Catalogue prices are planning estimates, not validated sales prices or a final offer. Preparation must not be charged twice if already included in a package.

Stage and evidence

SLEECH is an initiative research project by the Soavtory team and a B2B startup hypothesis. A prototype and software checks do not establish industrial validation, demand or fitness of the full measurement procedure.

No granted patent is confirmed. Novelty assessment and possible software registration are separate work. Development is documented through source versions, method descriptions and future trial records.
